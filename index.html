Drops soothe the surface.

We treat the cause.

Most dry eye is not a shortage of tears. It is tears that evaporate too fast because the tiny oil glands in your eyelids have clogged or become inflamed. Artificial tears help for an hour; they do nothing for the glands.

At DRY EYECARE we look at your tear film, lids and glands, explain what we find in plain language, and build a drop-less plan around in-office treatments that restore how your eyes make and keep their own moisture.

Is it dry eye?

Symptoms people put up with for years.

Dry eye rarely announces itself as "dry". If any of these sound familiar, especially more than one, an evaluation is worth your time.

Learn about dry eye → Book an evaluation →
  • Burning or stinging
  • A gritty, sandy feeling
  • Itchy, irritated eyes
  • Redness that keeps coming back
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Vision that blurs, then clears when you blink
  • Contact lenses that feel worse each year
  • Glare and strain driving at night
  • Eyes that feel tired by mid-afternoon
  • Watery eyes (often a sign of dryness, not too much moisture)

Why it happens

Find the cause, and the treatment follows.

Dry eye has many triggers, and two people with the same symptoms can need very different care. That is why every plan starts with a proper evaluation.

  • Meibomian gland dysfunction (blocked oil glands)
  • Blepharitis and Demodex mites
  • Hormonal changes
  • Allergies
  • Autoimmune conditions
  • Long hours on screens
  • Years of contact lens wear
  • Dry indoor heat and air conditioning
  • Recovery after LASIK or other eye surgery

Questions

Dry eye, answered

What happens at a dry eye evaluation?

We start with your symptoms and history, then look closely at your tear film, eyelids and oil glands. By the end of the visit you will know what is driving your dry eye and which treatments make sense for you, along with what they involve.

Will I still need eye drops?

The goal is to treat the cause so you rely on drops far less. Many patients use them only occasionally once their glands are working again; some still use a supportive drop as part of their plan.

Are the treatments painful?

The in-office treatments we use are non-invasive. Most patients describe a sensation of warmth, and you can return to your day straight afterwards.

How many treatments will I need?

It depends on the cause and how long you have had symptoms. Dry eye is usually treated as a short series of visits followed by maintenance, and we lay out the plan, including the number of sessions, before you start.

Which office should I visit?

Both. Dr. Lazar's team sees dry eye patients in Greenwich and in Darien, so choose whichever is closer or has the time that suits you. Call (203) 202-0202 and we will book either office.

Is DRY EYECARE part of PRO EYECARE?

Yes. DRY EYECARE is the dedicated dry eye practice of PRO EYECARE, with the same doctors, offices and phone number. For eye exams, glasses, contact lenses or myopia care, visit getproeyecare.com.

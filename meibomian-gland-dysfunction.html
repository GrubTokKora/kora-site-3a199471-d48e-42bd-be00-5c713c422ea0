Conditions we treat

Meibomian gland dysfunction treatment in Greenwich & Darien.

The meibomian glands line your eyelids and make the oil that keeps tears from evaporating. When they clog or become inflamed, the result is evaporative dry eye, the type we treat most.

What goes wrong in the glands

Healthy meibomian glands release a clear oil with every blink. In meibomian gland dysfunction, often shortened to MGD, that oil thickens, the openings of the glands block, and the surrounding tissue becomes inflamed.

Without enough oil, the tear film evaporates within seconds. Left untreated for years, blocked glands can shrink and stop working altogether, which is why we treat MGD early.

Signs of MGD

Meibomian gland dysfunction usually shows up as:

  • Burning, gritty eyes that are worse later in the day
  • Red or thickened eyelid margins
  • Vision that blurs and clears as you blink
  • Watery eyes, often with foamy tears
  • Recurring styes or chalazia
  • Contact lenses that feel worse than they used to

What contributes to it

Common contributors include:

  • Long hours on screens and incomplete blinking
  • Ocular rosacea and inflammation along the lids
  • Blepharitis and Demodex mites
  • Hormonal changes
  • Years of contact lens wear

How we treat it

The aim is to melt the thickened oil, clear the glands and calm the inflammation that keeps blocking them. Warm compresses alone rarely reach the temperature or consistency needed, which is where in-office treatment comes in.

Most plans combine two or three of the treatments below, chosen after we have examined your glands.

Questions

Common questions

Is MGD the same as dry eye?

MGD is the most common cause of dry eye, specifically the evaporative type. Not everyone with dry eye has MGD, which is why we examine the glands before recommending treatment.

Can blocked glands recover?

Glands that are blocked but still present often recover well with treatment. The earlier we start, the more gland function there is to protect.

Will warm compresses fix MGD?

They help as part of home care, but they cool quickly and rarely melt thickened oil fully. In-office heat and light treatments are far more effective.

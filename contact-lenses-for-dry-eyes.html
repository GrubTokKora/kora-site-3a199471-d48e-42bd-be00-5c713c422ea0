Contact lenses for dry eyes in Greenwich & Darien.

If your lenses feel scratchy by mid-afternoon, you are not alone. Dry eye is one of the most common reasons people give up contacts, and with the right lens and treatment, many do not have to.

Why lenses and dry eye clash

A contact lens sits in the tear film. If that film is thin or evaporates quickly, the lens dries out, the eye feels gritty and vision blurs. Soft lenses can also draw moisture away from the eye as the day goes on.

Lens options that can help

Depending on your eyes and prescription, options include:

  • Rigid gas-permeable (RGP) lenses, which draw less moisture from the eye than many soft lenses
  • Scleral lenses, which vault over the cornea and hold a reservoir of fluid against the eye
  • Specialty lenses for astigmatism, keratoconus and other conditions that complicate a fit

Treat the dryness, too

The best lens in the world will still feel dry on an unstable tear film. Treating the underlying cause, most often blocked oil glands, is usually what makes contact lenses comfortable again.

We look at both: your tear film and glands first, then the lens that suits your eyes. Your lens fitting itself is carried out by the PRO EYECARE team.

Questions

Common questions

Can I wear contacts if I have dry eye?

Often, yes. The right lens combined with treatment for the dryness lets many patients wear contacts comfortably again.

What are scleral lenses?

Larger rigid lenses that rest on the white of the eye and hold a layer of fluid over the cornea, which keeps the surface moist all day.

Should I stop wearing my lenses before my evaluation?

Call us when you book and we will tell you what to do before your visit, as it depends on your lens type.

Blepharitis treatment in Greenwich & Darien, CT.

Blepharitis is inflammation of the eyelids. It causes redness, itching, swelling and crusting along the lashes, and it often travels with dry eye and recurring styes.

What causes blepharitis

Blepharitis has several possible triggers, and treating the right one is what makes it settle:

  • Bacteria along the lash line, sometimes called staph blepharitis
  • Demodex mites multiplying in the lash follicles
  • Seborrheic dermatitis, the condition behind dandruff
  • Rosacea
  • Allergies
  • Blocked or poorly working oil glands

Symptoms

You may notice:

  • Red, swollen or itchy eyelids
  • Crusts or flakes at the base of the lashes
  • Burning, stinging or a gritty feeling
  • Watery eyes or foamy, bubbly tears
  • Greasy-looking lids
  • Sensitivity to light and blurry or fluctuating vision
  • Contact lenses that become hard to tolerate

Why it matters

Untreated blepharitis can lead to styes, chalazia, persistent redness and dry eye, as flakes and oil disrupt the tear film. In rarer cases it can cause lashes to grow in the wrong direction or fall out, or irritate the cornea.

How we treat it

We start by finding the cause, then combine in-office cleaning with a simple home routine. Depending on what we see, that can include prescription drops or ointment, targeted lid scrubs and warm compresses, alongside the in-office treatments below.

Questions

Common questions

Is blepharitis contagious?

No. It is an inflammatory condition of your own eyelids, not an infection you can pass on.

Will blepharitis go away on its own?

It tends to come and go, but it rarely resolves without treating the cause. Regular in-office cleaning and a good home routine keep it under control.

Are Demodex mites a sign of poor hygiene?

No. Everyone has them. Problems start only when they multiply, and that is very treatable.

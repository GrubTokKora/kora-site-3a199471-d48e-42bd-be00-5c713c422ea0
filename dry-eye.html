Conditions we treat

Dry eye in Greenwich & Darien, explained.

Dry eye is not just a shortage of tears. It is a problem with the quality, quantity or stability of the tear film, and it can range from a mild nuisance to symptoms that shape your whole day.

What is dry eye?

Each blink spreads a thin film of tears over the eye. That film has three layers: a mucus layer that helps it cling, a watery middle, and an outer layer of oil from the meibomian glands in your eyelids that stops the water evaporating.

Dry eye happens when that film breaks down. Most often the oil layer is the problem, so tears evaporate too quickly; this is called evaporative dry eye. Less often the eyes simply do not make enough of the watery layer.

Symptoms

Dry eye rarely feels just “dry”. The most common signs are:

  • Burning or stinging
  • A gritty, sandy feeling
  • Itchy, irritated eyes
  • Redness that keeps coming back
  • Sensitivity to light
  • Vision that blurs, then clears when you blink
  • Contact lenses that feel worse each year
  • Glare and strain driving at night
  • Eyes that feel tired by mid-afternoon
  • Watery eyes (often a sign of dryness, not too much moisture)

Common causes

Several things can upset the tear film, and many people have more than one:

  • Meibomian gland dysfunction (blocked oil glands)
  • Blepharitis and Demodex mites
  • Hormonal changes
  • Allergies
  • Autoimmune conditions
  • Long hours on screens
  • Years of contact lens wear
  • Dry indoor heat and air conditioning
  • Recovery after LASIK or other eye surgery

How we diagnose it

Every plan starts with a dedicated dry eye evaluation. We go through your symptoms, health, medications and screen habits, then look closely at your tear film, eyelids and meibomian glands.

That tells us whether inflammation, gland dysfunction, debris along the lashes or your environment is driving your symptoms, and that decides which treatment will actually help.

Habits that help between visits

Treatment does the heavy lifting, but small daily changes make a real difference:

  • Take regular breaks from screens and remember to blink fully
  • Add humidity to dry, heated or air-conditioned rooms
  • Stay well hydrated
  • Wear wraparound sunglasses in wind
  • Keep your lid-hygiene routine going once your plan is underway

When to see a doctor

If dryness, light sensitivity or blurred vision keep coming back, or you rely on eye drops every day, it is time for an evaluation. Earlier care usually means less inflammation and faster relief.

Questions

Common questions

Can dry eye be cured?

Dry eye is usually a chronic condition, but it can be controlled very well. Treating the cause, most often the oil glands, means most patients feel far better and need far fewer drops.

Why are my eyes watery if I have dry eye?

When the tear film is unstable, the eye senses irritation and floods itself with watery reflex tears. Those tears lack oil, so they evaporate or run off without fixing the problem.

Do screens cause dry eye?

Screens make us blink less and less completely, which lets tears evaporate. They rarely cause dry eye alone but often make it noticeably worse.

Dry eye treatments in Greenwich & Darien, CT.

Six in-office treatments, each aimed at a different cause of dry eye. Most patients do best with a combination, chosen by Dr. Lazar after a dedicated evaluation.

Compare

Six in-office treatments

Intense pulsed light

OptiLIGHT

Intense pulsed light that calms the inflammation behind meibomian gland dysfunction, the most common driver of dry eye.

About OptiLIGHT → Radiofrequency

OptiPLUS

FDA-cleared, dual-frequency radiofrequency that warms the tissue around the eyes to free up blocked oil glands.

About OptiPLUS → Targeted eyelid heat

TearCare

Soft, flexible heat patches warm your eyelids while your eyes stay open and you blink normally, so the oil inside your glands melts and can be cleared.

About TearCare → Photobiomodulation

Low-level light therapy

Gentle red and near-infrared LED light that reduces inflammation and supports healing around the eyelids.

About Low-level light therapy → Lid-margin exfoliation

BlephEx

A precise, in-office cleaning of the lash line that removes the bacteria, scurf and debris behind blepharitis.

About BlephEx → Eyelid cleansing

ZEST

A gentle in-office treatment that dissolves the biofilm, debris and Demodex mites that collect around the lashes.

About ZEST →

One plan,

built around the cause.

Inflammation and gland dysfunction respond best to OptiLIGHT, OptiPLUS, TearCare and low-level light therapy, which restore how the meibomian glands make and release oil.

Debris, bacteria and Demodex mites along the lashes are cleared with BlephEx or ZEST, often as the first step so that light and heat treatments can do their work.

We explain which treatments fit, how many visits they take and what you can do at home before anything begins.

How Low-level light therapy

works.

Low-level light therapy, also called photobiomodulation, uses LED light at specific wavelengths. The light is absorbed by the cells around the eyes, where it helps calm inflammation and supports the tissue as it heals.

It is gentler than OptiLIGHT and can be used where intense pulsed light is not suitable. Low-level light therapy is an off-label use for dry eye, but research is promising, and in our practice patients respond well, especially when it is combined with OptiLIGHT and gland expression.

Who it helps

Is Low-level light therapy right for you?

We recommend it after an evaluation, often alongside other treatments. It is typically considered for:

  • Meibomian gland dysfunction and evaporative dry eye
  • Blepharitis and inflamed eyelid margins
  • Chalazia and styes
  • Patients of any age or skin tone

What a session is like

You relax with your eyes closed while the LED light is applied around them. Most patients feel nothing beyond gentle warmth, and there is no recovery time.

It is often added to an OptiLIGHT visit, or used on its own for patients who are not candidates for intense pulsed light.

Questions

Low-level light therapy, answered

Is low-level light therapy FDA-approved for dry eye?

Its use for dry eye is off-label. Studies have been encouraging, and we explain the evidence and what to expect before recommending it.

Does it work for every skin tone?

Yes. Low-level light therapy is suitable for all ages and skin tones, which makes it a useful option when intense pulsed light is not.

Can it be combined with other treatments?

Yes. It is most often combined with OptiLIGHT and gland expression, and sometimes follows a BlephEx or ZEST cleaning.
